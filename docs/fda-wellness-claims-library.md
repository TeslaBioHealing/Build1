# 2026 FDA-Aligned General Wellness Claims Library

> Source: internal counsel doc `Claims_Library-Aligned-with-2026-FDA-_General_Wellness-Guidelines.docx`, provided 2026-09-22. Stored here so every future campaign, product page, or marketing draft across Tesla BioHealing / CellRenew / Healthy Night Habits / Healthy Sleep Space checks against it before publishing. Referred to as "FDA 2026 General-Wellness-Guidance-Aligned Claims" — **not** "FDA-approved claims"; the guidance never establishes that a product is safe or effective, only that certain wording stays inside the low-risk general-wellness category.

## The 3-tier tagging system

Tag every marketing sentence before it ships:

- 🟢 **GREEN — Approved Wellness Vocabulary**: Qi/energy flow, relaxation, everyday stress management, healthy sleep, sleep management, vitality, fitness, recovery, general well-being.
- 🟡 **YELLOW — Regulatory Review Required**: specific physiological mechanisms, measurable physiological changes, "clinically proven," cellular effects, circulation, oxygenation, metabolism, **biomarkers**, named chronic diseases, or unusually strong outcome claims.
- 🔴 **RED — Do Not Use for General-Wellness Marketing**: diagnose, treat, cure, mitigate, prevent, reverse, heal a disease; replace medication; substitute for medical treatment; medical-grade/clinical-grade claims; disease monitoring or treatment recommendations.

The central line FDA draws: general healthy-lifestyle functions vs. diagnosis, cure, mitigation, prevention, or treatment of disease. A product also has to stay low-risk in itself (generally noninvasive, no implant, no safety-critical tech) — wording alone doesn't qualify it.

### 5. Energy / Vitality / Fitness Claims
FDA accepts physical-fitness claims including improving energy, endurance, strength, coordination, and related fitness functions.

**Guidance:** favor "supports energy," "promotes vitality," "helps you feel refreshed" over physiological/mechanism claims such as **"increases mitochondrial ATP production"** unless separately evidenced and regulatory-reviewed.

### 6. Recovery Claims
The 2026 guidance allows wellness outputs to be contextualized to sleep, activity, stress, recovery, or similar domains.

### 9. Customer-Testimonial Language
Claims made through testimonials still count as advertising claims — vet them the same way.

**Lower-risk to quote:** "I feel more relaxed when I use it." · "It has become part of my nighttime wellness routine." · "I feel refreshed in the morning." · "I enjoy the sense of energy and relaxation." · "I feel like I sleep better." · "I feel more energized during the day." · "It helps me unwind before bedtime."

**Never publish without separate regulatory review:** "It cured my insomnia." · "I stopped taking sleeping pills." · "My chronic fatigue disappeared." · "It healed my neuropathy." · "It lowered my blood pressure." · "It treated my depression." · "It cured my pain."

Labeling, advertising, UI, and functionality all have to stay consistent with the general-wellness intended use — not just the headline copy.

## The wording distinction that matters most

| | Example |
|---|---|
| ✅ Excellent | "Promotes the flow of Qi ('energy')." — nearly a direct quote of FDA's own formulation |
| ⚠️ Problematic | "Restores the body's natural bioelectric energy and corrects cellular dysfunction." — asserts a specific physiological mechanism and "corrects dysfunction" |
| ✅ Excellent | "Supports healthy sleep." / "Promotes healthy sleep habits." / "Supports relaxation and sleep management." |
| ⚠️ Problematic | "Treats insomnia naturally without medication." — a disease/treatment claim, not general wellness |

**Practical takeaway used when editing copy:** strip out named biomarkers (e.g. CD34+), specific study statistics presented as outcome claims, and mechanism-of-action language ("ATP production," "cellular energy production," "corrects/restores/repairs X") from consumer marketing. Replace with the GREEN vocabulary above. Peer-reviewed research can be referenced in general terms, but the specific physiological finding itself is YELLOW — regulatory review required before it goes in marketing copy, not just a copywriting judgment call.
